HUMAN POSE / 17 FROZEN QUERIES / THREE CONTRASTS

Does the native pose recipe help?

The fixed native recipe uses person crops, joint heatmaps and flipped predictions. We compare all 17 queries against fresh generic CPU controls and the archived TPU outputs.

Verified: 2026-10-31T12:54:59.771843+00:00

This is a separate metadata-assisted adaptation to the MPII benchmark. It uses the demonstration’s joint identities and person grouping, plus a fixed external person detector on each query image. Query targets never condition predictions. The frozen benchmark scorer is unchanged; these are not the paper’s COCO keypoint AP results. This condition is excluded from the common RGB-only best-recipe selection.

The native pose condition improves the mean on these 17 queries. Against the fresh generic CPU control, mean PCK changes from 0.085294 to 0.620588: +0.535294 (+627.586%). 15 queries improve, 0 worsen and 2 tie. Support metadata, detection and pose decoding change together; this is not an isolated preprocessing fix.

PCK is higher-is-better. Positive changes mean improvement; negative changes mean regression. Relative changes can look large near zero, so inspect the absolute change too. Zero-baseline relative percentages are undefined. Scores are rounded here; downloads retain full precision.

All 17 queries are retained in every comparison. Gains and losses are each divided by 17, so their sum equals the mean change. Every copy control matches exactly across the three conditions.

The two new inference arms took 10.14 minutes on CPU. This does not establish TPU speed or numerical parity.

Recipe and scope

One support person chosen by most visible limbs, then largest visible-limb bounding area, then lowest person index. No query target use. Released17joint OKS NMS .9 with mean confidence above .2 times detector score; common12 limbs radius5; highest confidence person painted last; prediction-only episode recoding.

The adapter changes the human-pose route only. The other 12 categories were not rerun by this experiment; their existing results remain separate. There is no mixed best-recipe aggregate.

  • Separate from the common RGB-only benchmark condition.
  • Demonstration limb identities and person grouping are recovered from frozen support metadata; no query labels are read.
  • The fixed native pose adapter, support box policy and external detector are one preselected condition; their separate contributions are not identified.
  • The fixed benchmark pose scorer is retained; these scores are not the paper COCO keypoint AP.
  • No TPU numerical or performance qualification is established.

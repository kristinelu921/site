What is actually wrong

IMProv

  • Face landmarks decode only 2.4% of expected points on average; pose decodes 15.2%. The images are mostly blank or near-constant.
  • Colorization, deblurring, instance/semantic segmentation, and super-resolution visibly miss the target and score worse than copying the input.
  • The edge contrast experiment improves the raw result from 0.130808 to 0.327224, but remains slightly below the copy baseline.

InstaManip

  • Appearance outputs are often stylized or hallucinated; colorization, deblurring, inpainting, and super-resolution score worse than copying the input.
  • Segmentation outputs are noisy or over-segmented. The released fallback palette decoder replays correctly.
  • Edge, denoising, saliency, landmarks, pose, and normals are finite and behave as the images suggest.

Alternative-setting checks

Surface-normal convention

All 48 fixed axis/sign transforms were checked as a sensitivity test. IMProv moves from 86.89° to 84.74°; InstaManip moves from 82.21° to 77.24°. These small validation-selected gains do not establish a different released convention, so they are not promoted.

Saliency polarity

Reversing every prediction hurts both models: IMProv 0.489306 → 0.282926; InstaManip 0.521609 → 0.398993. The displayed polarity is correct.

Relative depth

The signed-scale rule already used by the main table was replayed. It changes IMProv and InstaManip only slightly; their remaining errors are visible prediction mismatches, not negative-scale zeroing.

Segmentation decoding

Neither model emits exact public-palette colors on these queries, so the declared released fallback decoder is used. Replaying it gives the published PQ and mIoU values exactly.
